Scoring Model
=============

    score(D, Q) = sum( idf(t) * (tf(t in D) * (k + 1)) /
                    (tf(t in D) + k * (1 - b + b * (|D| / avgdl)) )

    where:
        D = Document
        Q = Query
        t = Term in the query
        tf(t in D) = Term Frequency of term t in document D
        idf(t) = Inverse Document Frequency of term t
        |D| = Length of document D
        avgdl = Average document length in the index
        k and b = Tuning parameters (default: k=1.2, b=0.75)

Term Frequency Saturation
========================
    Term frequency saturation means:
        The importance of a term increases when it appears more times, but the benefit gradually levels off.
    In other words:
        first few occurrences → very important
        later occurrences → much less important

    It is handled by formula:
        tf(t in D) * (k + 1) / (tf(t in D) + k * (1 - b + b * (|D| / avgdl)) )



Field length normalization
==========================

    The section 1 - b + b * (|D| / avgdl) is called length normalization.
    It penalizes longer documents to prevent them from dominating the search results just because they have more terms.

    Longer documents are more likely to contain the query terms just by chance.
    To prevent this, BM25 includes a length normalization factor.
    This factor reduces the score of longer documents, ensuring that shorter, more relevant documents are not overshadowed.

    The length normalization factor is calculated as:
        1 - b + b * (|D| / avgdl)
    where:
        |D| = Length of the document D
        avgdl = Average document length in the index
        b = Tuning parameter (default: 0.75)

    The longer the document (|D|), the higher the length normalization factor, which reduces the overall score of the document.
    This helps to ensure that shorter, more relevant documents are ranked higher than longer documents that may contain more terms but are less relevant to the query.
